Privacy

What Fireflies stores, and what it doesn't

Last updated 15 August 2026

Fireflies keeps photos of meals you've eaten so you can rediscover them later. This page explains exactly what it stores and why. It is short because the app collects very little.

What Fireflies collects

  • Your meal photos — the images you choose to upload, and any tags you add to them.
  • An anonymous account identifier — created the first time you open the app so your meals stay yours between launches. It isn't linked to your name, email, phone number, or Apple ID, and you're never asked to register or sign in.
  • Your name, only if you type it — Fireflies lets you add a first name so the gallery can greet you. It stays on your device and is never sent anywhere.

What Fireflies doesn't collect

There is no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, and no tracking of any kind. Fireflies doesn't collect your location, your contacts, your device identifiers, or your browsing behaviour. Your data is never sold, never shared with third parties for marketing, and never used to build an advertising profile. There is no third-party tracking software in the app at all.

Where your data is stored

Your photos and meal details are stored using Google Firebase, which holds them on Google's servers. Access is restricted to your own anonymous account — no other person using Fireflies can read your meals.

Photo library access

Fireflies uses Apple's system photo picker, so it only ever receives the specific images you select. It has no access to the rest of your photo library.

Deleting your data

You can permanently delete everything from inside the app, under ⋯ → Account → Delete My Data. That removes your meal photos, your meal records, and your anonymous account. It can't be undone, and no backup copy is kept.

Children

Fireflies isn't directed at children and doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.

Support
